This follows your preferred order: morning jog with you, office at 11 AM, return around 7 PM, rest, dinner, rest, then gym.

Practical target: this schedule gives 8 hours of sleep while allowing a 1.5-hour gym session after dinner. A consistent 9 hours would require a shorter/earlier gym session or moving gym before dinner.

6:30–7:00 AMJog or brisk walk with his son at a comfortable pace.

7:30–8:00 AMBreakfast and prescribed morning medicine, following the prescription's food and timing instructions.
8:00–10:15 AMGet ready for work, family/construction check-in, and pack lunch and water.
10:15–11:00 AMCommute and settle in for office.
11:00 AM–6:30/7:00 PMOffice. Eat lunch at a reasonably regular time, drink water, and take brief movement breaks when possible.
6:30–7:00 PMReturn home as early as practical.
7:00–7:25 PMRest after work.
7:25–7:55 PMDinner. Follow his clinician's meal and medicine guidance.
7:55–8:15 PMQuiet rest before gym; avoid starting exercise while feeling overly full or unwell.
8:15–9:45 PMGym session (1–1.5 hours, depending on energy and medical advice). It does not need to be 1.5 hours every day.
9:45–10:10 PMCool down, shower, prepare essentials for tomorrow, and wind down.
10:15 PM–6:15 AMSleep: 8 hours. Keep bedtime and wake time steady.
To get closer to 9 hours: finish gym by 9:00 PM (about 45 minutes after dinner/rest), wind down, and aim to sleep by 9:15 PM; alternatively move gym before dinner. Do not routinely sacrifice sleep to extend workouts.
Diabetes and exercise: follow his clinician's advice on glucose checks and exercise. If his medicines can cause low glucose, ask how to prevent and treat lows around workouts. Stop if he feels unwell. Do not change medicine doses on your own.

Practical habits that can help glucose management

Choose sustainable changes; avoid extreme diet or exercise changes.

Build balanced Indian mealsInclude non-starchy vegetables, dal/beans and suitable protein; personalize rice/roti portions with a dietitian if possible.
Choose water instead of sugary drinksReduce sweetened tea/coffee, juice and soda; follow any fluid restriction from his clinician.
Move regularly, if safeA comfortable walk after meals may help. Follow clinician advice, especially if medicine can cause low glucose.
Keep medicine and monitoring consistentFollow prescribed doses and the recommended testing schedule; bring records to appointments.
Protect sleep and manage stressKeep a regular sleep routine and use realistic relaxation habits.
Foot care and check-upsCheck feet daily for cuts, blisters, swelling or colour changes; attend routine reviews as advised.
There is no guaranteed quick fix. Avoid “diabetes cures” and do not stop medication because a reading improves.

When to get help

Low glucose concern
If a reading is below 70 mg/dL or he has symptoms such as shaking, sweating, confusion or weakness, follow his clinician's low-glucose plan. If severe, unconscious, or unable to swallow, call emergency services and do not give food or drink by mouth.
Very high / unwell
Follow his clinician's sick-day plan. Seek urgent medical advice for very high readings with vomiting, abdominal pain, deep/rapid breathing, marked drowsiness, dehydration or confusion.
Appointment preparation
Bring readings and their timing, medication list, missed doses, symptoms and questions. Do not change doses based on this dashboard.
